# MSc-Economics-Projects

Applied econometrics and machine learning projects from my M.Sc. Economics at Trinity College Dublin — VAR/SVAR modelling, difference-in-differences, and ML classification in Stata and Python.

## Dissertation: ECB Monetary Policy Transmission to Sovereign Yields
Event-study regressions on the Euro Area Monetary Policy Event-Study Database (EA-MPD), testing whether ECB communication surprises transmit to sovereign bond yields differently depending on market uncertainty (VSTOXX, VIX) and fiscal vulnerability (debt-to-GDP), across Germany, France, Italy and Spain (2002–2025). **Tools:** Stata. **Finding:** market uncertainty dampens the yield response to ECB communication, while fiscal vulnerability amplifies it for forward-guidance surprises — two distinct transmission channels.

## Computational Methods Project
An end-to-end Python pipeline combining a difference-in-differences analysis with a machine-learning classification model to predict educational risk from panel survey data. Includes model comparison (logistic regression vs. random forest), hyperparameter tuning via GridSearchCV, stratified cross-validation, and evaluation via ROC-AUC and permutation importance. **Tools:** Python (pandas, scikit-learn, statsmodels).

## Econometrics II: Oil Volatility Shocks and US Macro-Financial Aggregates
Estimates a VAR(2) on post-2010 US data to trace how oil-price volatility shocks propagate to industrial production growth and S&P 500 returns, using orthogonalised impulse response functions, forecast error variance decomposition, and a recursive structural VAR as a robustness check. **Tools:** Stata.

## Econometrics I: MGNREGA and Child Schooling Progression in Rural India
A difference-in-differences analysis of India's national rural employment guarantee scheme (MGNREGA) using two waves of Indian Human Development Survey data (54,000+ children), testing whether the policy improved children's grade-for-age schooling progression, with a gender-heterogeneity extension. **Tools:** Stata.
